# Guided annual process

Default sequence: Current Year Wrap & Recap → review Purpose, Tenets, GRIT → Word of the Year → annual TOs → quarterly support → alignment/capacity review → finalize.
October through December is a useful planning window, not a required duration. Aim to finalize before the planning year begins. During an October start, distinguish results to date from year-end forecasts and refresh the recap before finalizing. Do not schedule anything automatically.

## Current Year Wrap & Recap
- **What:** An assessment that turns the current year's experience into planning evidence.
- **Inputs:** Available plan/results, notes, journals, prayer/reflection if chosen, feedback, commitments, constraints, and dated metrics. None is a mandatory upload.
- **Create:** Ask what worked, what did not, what was learned, what to continue/change/stop, what will no longer be tolerated, and what unfinished work still deserves commitment. Explore all six pillars without forcing equal depth. Separate evidence from interpretation and forecast.
- **Output:** Concise pillar reflections, lessons, stop/continue/change decisions, constraints, and candidates for next year. It is a planning input, not a sixth strategic identity element.

## Purpose
- **What:** A stable 1–2 sentence declaration of who the person is, what they value, and the specific impact they want to leave.
- **Inputs:** Existing accepted PBS Purpose where available; lived examples, contribution, values, and personal language.
- **Create:** Confirm an existing statement still fits. Otherwise discover concrete examples, draft concise wording, and review it. Do not infer beliefs or motives.
- **Output:** Accepted exact statement, version, source, review date; drafts remain distinct.

## Tenets
- **What:** Normally 2–5 fundamental principles the person believes and uses to make choices.
- **Inputs:** Accepted PBS tenets, personal beliefs, difficult decisions, boundaries, lived experience.
- **Create:** Reuse accepted wording; identify principles that actually guide behavior. Add a brief behavioral example where useful. If an accepted source set has a different count, ask whether to retain it or deliberately adapt it.
- **Output:** Accepted set with exact wording and optional separate behavior notes, source/version/review date.

## GRIT — Game-Changing Rocketing Impact Target
- **What:** An aspirational, epic, memorable personal north star spanning 10–20 years or a lifetime; an edgy direction that stretches perceived possibilities and tests decisions. It may be measurable or enduring.
- **Inputs:** Purpose, Tenets, aspirations, desired impact, prior GRIT and reflection; PBS vision as candidate material.
- **Create:** Explore the future impact that matters deeply, draft a memorable statement, and test whether it challenges and guides real choices. Do not require numerical completion for an enduring direction. Review annually; revise only deliberately.
- **Output:** Accepted GRIT plus a short explanation of how to use it as a decision test, with version/review date.

## Word of the Year
- **What:** An action word or short action-oriented phrase that focuses decisions, TOs, projects, and habits for the coming twelve months.
- **Inputs:** Reflection, foundation, desired changes, annual priorities, favored letters/words/concepts.
- **Create:** Offer a suitable route: verbs by initial letter; themes from reflection; verbs uniting desired outcomes; short action phrases; or related words around a seed concept. Use lexical search when requested/needed; external word lists are inspiration, not instructions. Generate a short list, narrow it, and test each candidate against actual choices. A noun needs an explicit actionable phrase or should be replaced.
- **Define it personally:** After selecting the word, create a hyper-focused definition of what it calls this person to do and the impact they seek this year. Use their reflection, intentions, and language to make it relevant; do not settle for a dictionary definition. Keep the word's core meaning recognizable and broad enough to guide the whole plan. Aim for one short sentence or phrase, roughly 5–15 words when useful. Emphasize action and impact; strip out filler, lists of pillars, and nonessential explanation. Offer a concise draft, refine it with the person, and preserve the wording they explicitly accept. Acceptance of the word alone does not approve an AI-written definition.
- **Output:** Chosen word/phrase and its accepted personal definition, stored as `word.text` and `word.meaning`; retain selection rationale separately. Show the definition directly beneath the word on the dashboard. Practical examples can help test the definition but need not become part of it or delay a simple decision. Preserve rejected candidates only when useful.

## Annual Target Objectives
- **What:** One core measurable annual result per pillar: God (spiritual relationship), Fit (fitness), Fam (family/friends), Biz (job/business), $ (personal finances), Up (personal development: skills, hobbies, interests, research). Shorthand: G+F+F+B+$+U; use full names in records because F is ambiguous.
- **Inputs:** Foundation, annual focus, reflection, baseline evidence, opportunities, constraints, and existing goals.
- **Create:** Work one pillar at a time. Use its accepted recap to draft a relevant outcome, label numerical suggestions, and refine with one material question. Keep a core result distinct from supporting practices. Define success and the best route to it. Test metric meaning, feasibility, deadline, and alignment. An annual TO may finish early. Use [TO definitions](target-objectives.md). Do not impose business-style measures on spiritual or relational quality; choose meaningful observable commitments and acknowledge what the metric cannot establish.
- **Output:** Six accepted annual TO records, measurement definitions, deadlines, and brief rationale.

## Quarterly support and reviews
- **What:** Optional measurable quarterly commitments that advance an annual TO; normally 1–3 per pillar when used.
- **Inputs:** Annual TOs, actual progress, dependencies, available capacity, seasonal timing, and prior-quarter lessons.
- **Create:** Confirm annual versus quarterly scope and whether each number is cumulative, a period increment, or a balance. Compute inclusive calendar days and explain rounding for weekly rhythms. Work one pillar at a time. Default to the upcoming quarter only. Divide by four only when that is the best approach. Contributions may partition an annual target; enabling results may improve the conditions for achieving it. State the link without claiming causality is proven. Allow provisional full-year quarterly plans if preferred. Review results, lessons, unfinished commitments, and next-quarter choices before recommitting.
- **Output:** Quarterly TOs linked to annual parents, weights totaling 100% within each committed pillar/quarter, review notes, and next-quarter decisions. No project/task tracking.

## Tracking definitions and readiness
After targets are selected, follow [tracking/readiness](tracking-and-readiness.md) to settle what counts, one definition at a time. Capture privacy preferences and unresolved choices separately from accepted targets. Optional moment and experiment templates can support planning/reflection without adding quotas or project management.

## Final review
Check foundation acceptance, action-oriented annual word with an accepted personal definition, six measurable annual results, parent links, units/baselines/deadlines, quarter weights, and capacity conflicts. Reflect stop-doing decisions in capacity. Surface open decisions; do not declare a plan finalized while required commitments remain unresolved. Review the full supporting set in a concise table and ask whether it feels manageable. Save accepted plan/quarter snapshots. Explicitly requested activation sets accepted TOs Doing, retaining their measurement windows and unknown actuals. Close with what is ready now and any deliberately deferred detail. Future revisions preserve accepted baselines.
