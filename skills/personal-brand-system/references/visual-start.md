# A simple starting look
Use when a leader asks to explore visual style or needs their first designed asset. This is a short working direction, not a prerequisite for interviews, text assets or using their brand.

## Start gently
Use known preferences and respect deferrals before asking anything. Ask: “Do you already have a look you'd like to use? You can share existing materials, start with a few preferences, or skip this for now. You don't need a logo or a complete design system.”

Confirm whether this asset should reflect the personal brand, organization brand or a deliberate combination. The workspace UI palette is not automatically the leader's public identity. Do not treat permission to use company branding as implied by employment.

## Track 1 — I have something
Invite whatever is available: colors, fonts, logo, icons, website or a reference they like. Partial materials are enough. Separate actual brand materials from inspiration. Reuse known decisions; ask only about gaps that matter for this asset. Record usage restrictions and font/asset availability when relevant. Do not require a complete brand pack or restart discovery because one item is missing.

## Track 2 — Help me find a starting point
Ask one question at a time, skipping answers already known:
- “What's a color you love—or definitely don't want?”
- “How would you like your work to feel: clean and confident, warm and approachable, or bold and energetic? You can describe it another way, too.”

Offer two simple directions on the same small example, using the intended asset when practical. Explain each in a sentence. Each needs one accent color, neutral backgrounds, readable text colors and one available font family with heading/body weights. Pick font candidates that suit the stated feeling; explain their effect in everyday words rather than presenting a font catalog. The AI checks text contrast and font availability, and provides a fallback when needed. Keep samples clearly provisional. If rendering is unavailable, show a compact comparison and disclose that the visual preview has not been made.

Use the person's name as text. A logo, custom icons, image generation, mood board and full design system are not requirements. Offer them later only for a real need or explicit interest. Do not start paid generation or acquire assets just to complete this exercise.

## Keep it bounded
Aim for roughly five minutes of active participation, two initial questions and one comparison. This is a pacing guide, not a timer cutoff or a new completion target. Invite a choice, one adjustment, or deferral. If they remain unsure, offer a neutral provisional direction and proceed only within their requested work. Explore further when the leader explicitly wants to; do not turn a quick asset request into a branding workshop.

## Save and learn through use
Save a compact “Working visual direction” in the personal KB or visual asset: context (personal/company/combination), liked/disliked references, accent and neutral colors, font/fallback, usage restrictions, decision status (proposed/accepted-for-use/deferred), date and next real use. Distinguish leader preferences from AI suggestions and keep undecided items visible. Do not add a new onboarding checklist or mark visual work accepted from an AI self-check.

Apply it to a real asset, check readability and visual fit, then ask one concise review question alongside the existing accuracy/voice/delivery review. Learn from actual edits. A working direction can evolve; retain prior accepted choices and propose material changes rather than silently replacing them. Public use still follows the asset's review and publication permissions.
