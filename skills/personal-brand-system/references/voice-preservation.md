# Preserve the leader's voice
Version 0.1 | 2026-10-13 | Use for synthesis, style-guide creation and asset drafting.

## Start with source language
Read relevant original answers and writing alongside the current guide. Weight current, self-written material and explicit preferences more than old corporate copy or AI-assisted prose. Preserve originals, provenance and full stories separately from edited outputs. Interview speech can support voice discovery when writing samples are unavailable; do not demand uploads before helping.

Keep a small source phrase bank: the leader's memorable expressions, metaphors, preferred verbs, humor and ways of explaining. Record exact excerpt, source/date/context, disclosure and whether it is current or historical. Do not impose a phrase quota or repeat signatures everywhere.

## Build evidence-based rules
Each voice rule should include: trait, exact source example, practical writing instruction, confidence and accepted correction where available. Distinguish observed behavior from desired style. Few examples mean provisional rules, not a confident personality verdict. Separate workplace tone from personal identity; a company voice does not overwrite the person's voice.

Keep selected raw excerpt → lightly edited version → leader feedback examples. Feedback such as “too corporate” becomes a concrete rule only after identifying the offending wording and preferred alternative. Store confirmed corrections and update affected future work.

## Draft and check
1. Use relevant stories, specific details and the leader's own framing before adding connective prose. Direct quotation stays exact; label paraphrases and preserve uncertainty.
2. Edit lightly when the source already works. Correct transcription noise and errors without sanding away personality. Preserve meaningful quirks and natural rhythm; adapt to audience and format.
3. Remove unsupported praise, inflated impact, generic inspiration, corporate filler and repeated canned sentence patterns. Do not add fake anecdotes, feelings, opinions, quotes, deliberate mistakes or forced slang to sound human.
4. Compare the draft to its sources. Check claims, attribution and important nuances. Ask: could this paragraph belong unchanged to almost any leader? If so, replace generic language with supplied specifics or omit it. Do not invent detail to pass this check.
5. Read it aloud for voice fit. Keep contractions, humor, punctuation and sentence rhythm when supported by the leader and channel. Do not apply universal punctuation bans or random variation. Explicit technical-language requirements take precedence for the requested asset.
6. Offer one short passage for calibration: “Does this sound like you? Which words would you change?” Avoid another long questionnaire. Keep accepted parts and change only what needs work.

Internal KB summaries can use neutral third person. Public first-person assets should reflect the person's reviewed voice. Mark unreviewed interpretations; don't present generic AI-written identity claims as the person's accepted self-description.

## Quality check, not authorship detection
Assess source fidelity, specificity, voice fit and usefulness with concrete examples. Do not promise undetectability, equate style patterns with proof of authorship, or send private samples to external detectors. No AI-likeness score is needed. Source-preserving editorial principles are integrated here; no external skill installation or service is required.
